📖 Theory & Concepts

What is Cheminformatics?

Cheminformatics (also known as chemical informatics or chemoinformatics) is an interdisciplinary field that combines chemistry, computer science, and information technology to collect, store, analyze, and manipulate chemical data.

Key Areas:

  • Molecular Representation: Encoding molecular structures in computer-readable formats (SMILES, InChI, MOL files)
  • Molecular Descriptors: Numerical values that describe molecular properties (LogP, TPSA, MW)
  • Structure-Activity Relationships (SAR): Understanding how molecular structure affects biological activity
  • Virtual Screening: Computational methods to identify promising drug candidates
  • ADMET Prediction: Predicting Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, and Toxicity

Applications:

  1. Drug Discovery: Identifying and optimizing lead compounds
  2. Materials Science: Designing new materials with desired properties
  3. Environmental Chemistry: Predicting environmental fate of chemicals
  4. Chemical Safety: Toxicity prediction and risk assessment

SMILES (Simplified Molecular Input Line Entry System)

SMILES is a line notation for representing molecular structures as text strings. It was developed by David Weininger in the late 1980s.

Basic Rules:

Symbol Meaning Example
Uppercase lettersAtomsC, N, O, S, P
Lowercase lettersAromatic atomsc, n, o
=Double bondC=O (formaldehyde)
#Triple bondC#N (hydrogen cyanide)
()BranchesCC(=O)O (acetic acid)
NumbersRing closuresc1ccccc1 (benzene)
[]Complex atoms[Na+], [Fe+2]
/, \Cis/trans stereochemistryF/C=C/F
@, @@Chirality[C@@H]

Molecular Descriptors

Molecular descriptors are numerical values that characterize properties of molecules. They are fundamental in cheminformatics for quantitative structure-activity relationships (QSAR).

Key Descriptors:

Descriptor Description Drug-like Range
Molecular Weight (MW)Sum of atomic masses≤ 500 g/mol
LogPPartition coefficient (octanol/water)≤ 5
TPSATotal Polar Surface Area≤ 140 Å²
H-Bond Donors (HBD)NH and OH groups≤ 5
H-Bond Acceptors (HBA)N and O atoms≤ 10
Rotatable BondsMolecular flexibility≤ 10
Fraction Csp3Saturation measureHigher is more 3D-like
Molar RefractivityPolarizability & volume40–130

Lipinski's Rule of 5:

A molecule is likely orally active if it satisfies:

  • MW ≤ 500
  • LogP ≤ 5
  • HBD ≤ 5
  • HBA ≤ 10

Violations of ≥ 2 rules suggest poor oral bioavailability.

3D Molecular Models

Different visualization styles highlight different aspects of molecular structure:

1. Ball and Stick Model

Atoms shown as small spheres, bonds as sticks. Best for seeing both atom identity and bond connectivity. Most commonly used in chemistry education.

2. Stick Model

Only bonds are shown as cylindrical sticks. Atoms are at the intersection points. Best for viewing molecular geometry and bond angles.

3. Space Filling (CPK) Model

Atoms shown at their van der Waals radii. Best for understanding molecular shape and surface. Shows how much space the molecule actually occupies.

4. Wireframe Model

Bonds shown as simple lines. Most minimal representation. Best for large molecules where detail would be overwhelming.
